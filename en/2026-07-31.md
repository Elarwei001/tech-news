## Headline Deep Dive

### Claude Breaks Cryptography — Anthropic's Mythos Model Finds Flaws in Post-Quantum Signature Scheme and AES

**Event**: On July 28, Anthropic published research showing that Claude Mythos Preview discovered improved attacks against two major cryptographic algorithms. The first attack significantly weakens HAWK, a digital signature scheme under consideration by NIST for post-quantum standardization (Round 3 candidate). Despite surviving two rounds of expert human review over two years, Mythos found a previously unexploited symmetry in HAWK's lattice structure — a nontrivial automorphism — that cuts its effective key size in half. The second result is a new attack on round-reduced AES that improves the speed of the previous best attacks by 200-800×.

Neither result breaks production systems today. HAWK is not yet deployed, and the AES attack targets a weakened variant. But both demonstrate that frontier AI models can now perform original cryptanalysis — finding mathematical flaws in algorithms, not just implementation bugs.

The HAWK attack was discovered semi-autonomously over 60 hours, with one Anthropic researcher providing project management-level guidance while Mythos agents conducted literature review, mathematical reasoning, computational experiments, and verification. The AES attack was found fully autonomously using a scaffold built by a different researcher. Each result cost approximately $100,000 in API compute.

Anthropic also partnered with ETH Zurich, Tel Aviv University, and TU Berlin to release CryptanalysisBench, a benchmark for evaluating LLM cryptanalytic capabilities. They followed responsible disclosure: the HAWK finding was shared with its authors in June and simultaneously disclosed to the public NIST mailing list.

**Link**: [Original](https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses)

**Why it matters**: This is the first public demonstration that a frontier AI model can discover novel mathematical weaknesses in cryptographic algorithms — not just find bugs in code implementations. HAWK passed two rounds of NIST review by expert cryptographers. Mythos found the attack in 60 hours. The implication for security infrastructure worldwide is profound: AI is becoming an essential tool for stress-testing the cryptographic foundations of the internet, and possibly a threat to systems that haven't been AI-vetted.

**Analysis**: Three takeaways deserve attention.

First, the multi-agent discovery dynamics are remarkable. The key idea for the HAWK attack was found by a pair of worker agents collaborating: the first prematurely rejected the approach as infeasible, but the second persisted and found a way to exploit it fully. This mirrors how human research teams work — and suggests that multi-agent setups may consistently outperform single-agent configurations for open-ended research.

Second, the cost-to-impact ratio is staggering. $100,000 in API compute produced a result that eluded the international cryptography community for two years. If this pattern holds across other security domains, it fundamentally changes the economics of defensive research — and offensive research.

Third, CryptalysisBench is a strategic move. By creating a public benchmark for LLM cryptanalysis, Anthropic is both inviting scrutiny of this capability and setting the terms of the conversation. Expect every frontier lab to report CryptanalysisBench scores within months — and expect policy makers to start asking hard questions about who gets access to models this capable at breaking math.

## Anthropic

### Project Pilot: AI Models Learn to Fly Drones — Drone-Bench Benchmark Reveals Rapid Capability Gains

**Event**: On July 24, Anthropic's Frontier Red Team, in collaboration with Andon Labs, published Project Pilot — a research project evaluating whether AI models can autonomously control drones for surveillance tasks. The team created Drone-Bench, a benchmark decomposing drone surveillance into five sub-tasks: Reconstruct (3D model from video), Localize (match drone view to map), Navigate (plan and fly paths), Detect (find target person from reference photo), and Follow (track target).

Testing 15 models from three developers, results show a clear capability trajectory. Claude Fable 5 — the best performer — passed the baseline on four of five tasks, failing only at Reconstruct (converting video into accurate 3D models). When tested end-to-end on a real drone, Fable 5 detected and followed targets noticeably better than the reference algorithm, but couldn't navigate between rooms due to compounding reconstruction errors.

A telling finding: the frontier of what models can do is about six months ahead of what they do consistently. Fable 5's average performance today matches the best one-off performance of models from early 2026.

**Link**: [Original](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-pilot)

**Analysis**: The decomposition methodology is the real contribution here. By breaking "can AI fly a drone?" into five measurable sub-tasks, Anthropic has created a framework that makes capability progress legible and predictable. The finding that only Reconstruct is blocking end-to-end autonomous surveillance is both reassuring (there's still a bottleneck) and alarming (once it falls, everything falls). The dual-use framing — drones used for crop monitoring and warfare — is exactly the kind of nuance policy discussions need.

## OpenAI

### GPT-5.6 Gets Massive Price Cuts: Luna Down 80%, Terra Down 20%, Plus New Fast Mode

**Event**: On July 30, OpenAI announced significant price reductions for the GPT-5.6 family. GPT-5.6 Luna — the fastest, most affordable model — now costs 80% less. GPT-5.6 Terra — the balanced workhorse — costs 20% less. New API pricing: Luna at $0.20/1M input and $1.20/1M output tokens; Terra at $2/1M input and $12/1M output tokens. Sol pricing is unchanged.

OpenAI also introduced Fast mode in the API, replacing Priority Processing. For GPT-5.6 Sol, Fast mode delivers up to 2.5× faster speeds than Standard processing at twice the price, with no change in intelligence. Existing API requests tagged "priority" will automatically use Fast mode.

The company framed these cuts as a direct result of the efficiency gains detailed in their previous day's engineering blog post — where GPT-5.6 Sol autonomously optimized its own inference pipeline, reducing serving costs by 20% and improving token generation efficiency by 15%.

**Link**: [Original](https://openai.com/index/advancing-the-price-performance-frontier-with-gpt-5-6/)

**Analysis**: This is OpenAI weaponizing its efficiency flywheel. Luna at roughly 6 cents on the dollar per task compared to frontier-class models from a year ago, at nearly 9× the speed, fundamentally changes what's economical to run at scale. On Agents' Last Exam, Luna outperforms Fable 5 at an estimated cost per task nearly 99% lower — a gap so wide it effectively creates a new market tier below "affordable frontier."

The Fast mode introduction is equally strategic. By offering 2.5× speed at 2× price, OpenAI is segmenting the market: pay more when latency matters, pay less when it doesn't. This is classic cloud-infrastructure pricing strategy applied to intelligence — and it's a moat that compounds with scale.

### Scientific Computing in the Age of Agentic AI — Field Report on Eight Research Projects

**Event**: On July 28, OpenAI published a field report documenting eight agent-assisted scientific computing projects, primarily in the life sciences. Five used Codex alone; three used a combination of Codex and Claude Code. Projects ranged from modernizing genomic data parsing libraries (cyvcf2) to GPU-native rewrites of bioinformatics tools.

The report identifies a consistent pattern: agents significantly accelerated development, but the bottleneck shifted from implementation to verification. Researchers' roles evolved from coding to orchestration — specifying what to build, defining correctness criteria, and judging results. Agents often expressed confidence even when their work contained errors, making human validation essential.

A key finding for the research community: lower implementation costs risk fragmenting the ecosystem. When rewriting tools becomes cheap, many similar rewrites proliferate, spreading the expert attention needed for maintenance. Long-term stewardship — not technical capability — becomes the binding constraint.

**Link**: [Original](https://openai.com/index/scientific-computing-agentic-ai/)

**Analysis**: This is one of the most honest assessments of AI-assisted research to date. The observation that "the last mile often took the most work" cuts against the hype narrative of effortless AI coding. And the warning about ecosystem fragmentation — "today's modern rewrite can become tomorrow's abandoned code" — is exactly the kind of systemic risk that the scientific community isn't yet discussing. The field report format, with case studies written by the actual research teams, gives this credibility that vendor marketing lacks.

## Google DeepMind

### Gemini Robotics 2: Whole-Body Intelligence for Humanoids, Multi-Robot Collaboration, and On-Device Adaptation

**Event**: On July 30, Google DeepMind announced Gemini Robotics 2, a major upgrade to its physical AI platform. The release includes three models:

- **Gemini Robotics 2 (VLA)**: A vision-language-action model capable of controlling entire humanoid robots — from feet to fingertips — including walking, crouching, and object manipulation. It enables dexterous actions like tying knots and sealing ziplock bags using 22-DOF five-fingered hands.
- **Gemini Robotics ER 2**: An embodied reasoning model that serves as the robot's high-level brain, planning multi-step tasks lasting several minutes. Crucially, it introduces multi-robot collaboration — different robot types communicating and working together.
- **Gemini Robotics On-Device 2**: An efficient VLA that runs locally on robotic hardware and can adapt to entirely new robot embodiments with just a few hours of data (~200 examples), even with drastically different shapes and sensor configurations.

DeepMind also introduced ASIMOV-Agentic, a new safety benchmark for agentic safety orchestration, measuring whether embodied reasoning agents can refuse unsafe tool calls and request human intervention when uncertain. Gemini Robotics ER 2 is described as DeepMind's safest robotics model to date in safety constraint following and human proximity benchmarks.

Gemini Robotics ER 2 is available on Google AI Studio and in private preview on Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform. VLA and On-Device models are available to early-access partners.

**Link**: [Original](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-brings-whole-body-intelligence-to-robots/)

**Analysis**: Three elements distinguish this release. First, whole-body control — not just arms and hands, but locomotion — represents a meaningful step toward general-purpose humanoid robots. The ability to ask Apollo 2 to "put the watering can into the green bin on the bottom shelf" and watch it walk, pick up, navigate, and place the object is a genuine milestone.

Second, the on-device adaptation story is powerful. Adapting to a completely new robot body in hours with less than 200 examples means the barrier to deploying AI on novel hardware is dropping fast. This matters because the robotics hardware landscape is fragmented — if you need years of data per platform, you can't scale.

Third, the ASIMOV-Agentic safety benchmark addresses the right question: not just "can the robot do the task?" but "can the reasoning layer refuse unsafe actions from the action layer?" This separation of concerns — safety orchestration as an independent checkpoint — is a design pattern that the entire robotics industry should adopt.

## Trend Watch

**AI is becoming its own research engine.** Anthropic's cryptographic discoveries, OpenAI's self-optimizing inference pipeline, and Google DeepMind's AI-driven robotics safety benchmarks all point to the same meta-trend: frontier AI models are increasingly doing the research work that improves AI itself. The flywheel is spinning — better models discover better optimizations and find better designs, which produce better models. The question is no longer "can AI do research?" but "how long until AI-driven research outpaces human-driven research in most domains?"

**Price wars are intensifying at the bottom.** OpenAI's 80% Luna price cut and Google's 3.5 Flash-Lite at $0.30/1M input tokens signal that the race for high-volume, low-cost inference is entering a new phase. The strategic implication: companies without an efficiency flywheel (Cheaper models → more usage → more data → better models → cheaper models) will be squeezed out. Expect consolidation among mid-tier model providers within 12-18 months.

**Physical AI is accelerating faster than expected.** Gemini Robotics 2's whole-body humanoid control, Anthropic's Drone-Bench showing models six months from autonomous surveillance, and the general trajectory of embodied AI all suggest that the "robots in the real world" timeline is compressing. The combination of multi-embodiment adaptation (hours, not months) and agentic safety orchestration means the remaining barriers are more about deployment infrastructure than capability.
