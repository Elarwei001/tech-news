OpenAI introduced its first custom inference chip, Google built computer use into Gemini 3.5 Flash, and Anthropic kept pushing agents into team collaboration.

## Headline Deep Dive

### OpenAI and Broadcom's Jalapeño Shows Inference Cost Is Now a Frontier-AI Battleground

**Event**: On June 24, OpenAI and Broadcom unveiled Jalapeño, OpenAI's first Intelligence Processor optimized for LLM inference. OpenAI says Jalapeño is the first step in a multi-generation compute platform built with Broadcom and Celestica. Engineering samples are running ML workloads in the lab at target frequency and power, including GPT-5.3-Codex-Spark; early testing indicates substantially better performance per watt than current state-of-the-art systems. OpenAI also says the chip moved from design to production tape-out in nine months and is intended for gigawatt-scale deployment with data center partners across multiple generations.

**Links**: [OpenAI original](https://openai.com/index/openai-broadcom-jalapeno-inference-chip/) | [Chinese translated digest](../translations/2026-06-25/openai-broadcom-jalapeno.md) *(AI translated digest)*; [Broadcom announcement](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/openai-and-broadcom-unveil-llm-optimized-intelligence-processor)

**Why it matters**: If the center of AI competition in 2024 and 2025 was model capability, the center in 2026 is becoming who can deliver models to users more cheaply, reliably, and with lower latency. Jalapeño is not only a story about OpenAI making a chip. It is a signal that OpenAI wants to optimize inference platforms, model roadmaps, kernels, serving systems, product needs, and data center deployment together.

This is different from the training-chip race. Inference happens in every ChatGPT response, every Codex task, every API call, and every future agent step. As agents become longer-running, more tool-heavy, and more asynchronous, inference cost and latency are magnified. A model may answer one question in seconds; an agent may plan, retrieve, read files, write code, call tools, verify results, and repair mistakes repeatedly. Every step burns tokens, power, and scheduling capacity.

**Analysis**: The important part of Jalapeño is OpenAI's new definition of full stack. Full stack used to mean models, products, and APIs. Now it extends to chip architecture, memory movement, networking, scheduling, data centers, and supply chains. OpenAI's emphasis on reducing data movement, balancing compute/memory/networking, and pushing realized utilization closer to theoretical peak shows that the bottleneck is no longer simply "more GPUs." It is how much usable intelligence can be produced per watt, per rack, and per interaction.

That creates two pressures. First, NVIDIA remains central to training and general-purpose acceleration, but large model companies will increasingly want custom hardware for their own inference traffic. Second, enterprise customers will not feel the chip name; they will feel response speed, reliability, price, and capacity. OpenAI's choice to start with an inference accelerator is pragmatic: training still depends on extremely general, large-scale ecosystems, while inference is easier to optimize around a company's own workloads.

The open questions remain real. OpenAI has not yet published a full performance report, and Jalapeño's production ramp, yield, software ecosystem, supply chain, and true TCO still need proof. What is clear today is the direction: frontier AI's bottleneck is shifting from model parameters alone to physical infrastructure. Winning AI companies will need not only to train models, but to turn those models into scalable, affordable, sustainable services.

## OpenAI

### Company: Jalapeño Takes OpenAI's Infrastructure Strategy Down to Silicon

**Event**: OpenAI calls Jalapeño its first Intelligence Processor and says it is designed around inference needs across ChatGPT, Codex, the API, and future agentic products. Broadcom contributes silicon implementation and networking, while Celestica contributes board, rack, and system integration.

**Link**: [Original](https://openai.com/index/openai-broadcom-jalapeno-inference-chip/)

**Analysis**: OpenAI is acting more like a hyperscaler than a pure model company. It is trying to merge the model roadmap with the infrastructure roadmap: model needs shape chip design, and chip efficiency shapes the scale at which models can be served.

### Security: Daybreak Continues the Governance Path for High-Permission Agents

**Event**: This week OpenAI introduced Daybreak and Patch the Planet, covering Codex Security, trusted access to GPT-5.5-Cyber, partner distribution, and collaboration with open-source maintainers.

**Links**: [Daybreak](https://openai.com/index/daybreak-securing-the-world/) / [Patch the Planet](https://openai.com/index/patch-the-planet/)

**Analysis**: Jalapeño addresses the supply-side cost of intelligence. Daybreak addresses the operating boundaries for high-capability agents. Together, they show OpenAI filling in both infrastructure and governance layers.

## Google DeepMind / Google AI

### Models: Gemini 3.5 Flash Gets Built-In Computer Use

**Event**: Google announced on June 24 that computer use is now a built-in tool in Gemini 3.5 Flash. Previously available as a standalone Gemini 2.5 computer use model, the capability is now integrated into the main Gemini Flash model. Developers can use Gemini API and Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform to build custom agents that can see, reason, and act across browser, mobile, and desktop environments. Google also introduced optional enterprise safeguards: requiring explicit user confirmation for sensitive or irreversible actions, and automatically stopping tasks when indirect prompt injection is detected.

**Link**: [Original](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-computer-use-gemini-3-5-flash/)

**Analysis**: Google is moving agent action capability from a special-purpose model into a mainstream model. The meaningful part is not browser automation alone, but the safety default. Once computer use enters enterprise workflows, prompt injection, irreversible actions, and permission boundaries will determine usability as quickly as raw model intelligence.

### Responsibility & Safety: AI Control Roadmap Frames Agent Security

**Event**: Google DeepMind recently published its AI Control Roadmap, which treats internal agents as potential insider threats and uses supervisors, detection, prevention, coverage, recall, and time-to-response to assess system-level security. Google says it has analyzed one million coding agent tasks to improve live monitoring for Gemini Spark agent.

**Link**: [Original](https://deepmind.google/blog/securing-the-future-of-ai-agents/)

**Analysis**: This is the other side of Gemini 3.5 Flash computer use. If agents can operate computers, companies need systems that can monitor, block, and replay agent behavior.

## Anthropic

### Product: Claude Tag Puts Claude Into Slack Team Channels

**Event**: Anthropic introduced Claude Tag beta on June 23 for Claude Enterprise and Team customers. Teams can @Claude inside Slack channels, grant it access to selected channels, tools, data, and codebases, and delegate tasks. Anthropic says its internal version of Claude Tag created 65% of its product team's code.

**Link**: [Original](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-tag)

**Analysis**: Claude Tag is about multiplayer collaboration, channel memory, asynchronous execution, spend limits, and audit logs. It shows that enterprise agent competition is moving from individual productivity to how organizations can safely delegate work.

### Research: Claude Code Usage Research Shows Expertise Still Pays

**Event**: Anthropic recently published research on Claude Code usage, based on a privacy-preserving analysis of around 400,000 interactive sessions from about 235,000 users.

**Link**: [Original](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise)

**Analysis**: This gives useful context to Claude Tag. Agents can expand execution capacity, but experts still create key value through task decomposition, context judgment, review, and risk control.

## NVIDIA

### Physical AI: NVIDIA Publishes GPU Optimization Work for BEV Pooling

**Event**: NVIDIA published a June 24 technical post on accelerating bird's-eye-view pooling on GPUs for autonomous vehicles, robotics, and spatial AI. BEV perception projects multi-view sensing into a top-down spatial representation, a common design pattern for autonomous systems.

**Link**: [Original](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerating-bev-pooling-on-nvidia-gpus-for-physical-ai-applications/)

**Analysis**: Appearing on the same day as OpenAI's chip announcement, this shows two infrastructure tracks. OpenAI is building custom LLM inference silicon; NVIDIA is pushing its GPU software stack deeper into robotics, autonomous driving, and spatial intelligence operators.

### Omniverse: Physical AI Capabilities for Existing Applications

**Event**: NVIDIA introduced Omniverse Libraries on June 23 for teams that want to embed physical AI capabilities into existing applications, with emphasis on simulation, robot design validation, and industrial workflows.

**Link**: [Original](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/integrate-physical-ai-capabilities-into-existing-apps-with-nvidia-omniverse-libraries/)

**Analysis**: As AI moves from text agents into robotics and industrial systems, simulation, digital twins, and physical consistency become a second infrastructure stack beyond the model.

## Mistral AI

### Document AI: Mistral OCR 4 Emphasizes Cost and Latency

**Event**: Mistral released OCR 4 for enterprise document AI, with support for 170 languages, bounding boxes, block classification, inline confidence scores, and self-hosted deployment. Mistral says that on a chart- and figure-dense financial QA dataset, OCR 4 reached comparable accuracy to leading agentic document parsers at roughly 8x lower cost and 17x lower latency.

**Link**: [Original](https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/)

**Analysis**: OCR 4 and Jalapeño are really talking about the same commercial problem. Enterprise AI deployments are not only asking whether something works. They ask what it costs per thousand pages, per thousand tokens, and per task, and whether it can run under local deployment and compliance constraints.

## Trend Watch

### AI Competition Is Moving From Model Leaderboards to Unit Economics and Control Planes

Today's news points to a more practical industry phase.

OpenAI's Jalapeño pushes inference efficiency down to silicon. Google builds computer use into Gemini 3.5 Flash while emphasizing safeguards for prompt injection and irreversible actions. Anthropic places Claude inside Slack channels, wrapped in permissions, memory, logs, and spend controls. NVIDIA continues serving physical AI through GPU software. Mistral uses OCR 4 to emphasize document-AI cost, latency, and self-hosting.

That means the second half of 2026 will not be judged only by whose model is stronger. The key questions will be who can serve more inference within a lower power envelope, who can let agents safely operate real software, who can put memory and permissions inside organizational boundaries, and who can push cost down to enterprise-budget levels.
