## Headline

### When AI Builds Itself: Anthropic Discloses Internal Data on Recursive Self-Improvement

**Event**: On June 6, the Anthropic Institute published [When AI builds itself](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/recursive-self-improvement), a long-form piece that constitutes the most detailed public disclosure to date by any AI company on the topic of recursive self-improvement — the growing role of AI systems in developing their own successors. Drawing on both public benchmark data and previously unreported internal Anthropic data, the article paints a clear picture of AI accelerating AI development.

**Key Data**:
- **80% of Anthropic's code is written by Claude**: As of May 2026, over 80% of the code merged into Anthropic's codebase was authored by Claude. This figure was in the low single digits before Claude Code launched in February 2025
- **Engineer output up 8x**: In Q2 2026, the typical engineer was merging 8× as much code per day as in 2024. Lines of code is an imperfect metric, but the increase aligns with subjective impressions of large productivity gains
- **Open-ended task success rate jumped from 26% to 76%**: In six months, Claude's success rate on the most complex, underspecified tasks improved by 50 percentage points
- **Experiment optimization: from 3x to 52x**: In Anthropic's internal benchmark (optimize a small model-training script for speed), Claude Opus 4 achieved ~3x speedup in May 2025. By April 2026, Claude Mythos Preview reached ~52x. For calibration, a skilled human researcher would need 4–8 hours to reach 4x
- **Autonomous research reaches 97% of human performance**: In an April 2026 AI safety research project, Claude-powered agents recovered 97% of the performance gap between weak and strong supervisors, while two human researchers recovered only 23% over about a week
- **Self-reported 4x productivity boost**: A poll of 130 Anthropic research employees found the median respondent estimated producing around 4× as much output with Mythos Preview as without AI assistance

**Context**: Recursive self-improvement — an AI system fully autonomously designing and developing its own successor — has long been a theoretical concept in AI safety discourse. Anthropic is clear that "we are not there yet" and that "recursive self-improvement is not inevitable." But the data presented shows the trend line moving fast toward that threshold.

The article traces Anthropic's internal evolution: 2021–2023 humans writing code → 2023–2025 chatbots assisting with snippets → 2025–2026 coding agents writing entire files → today's autonomous agents running code and delegating hours of work to other agents. The next step: "closing the loop," where agents are capable enough to build and train models themselves.

**Why It Matters**: This article is notable not because its conclusion is surprising — we knew AI was getting increasingly useful for coding and research — but because of the granularity and transparency of the data. 80% of code, 8× output, 76% open-ended task success — these are first-party numbers from one of the world's most advanced AI labs. And Anthropic doesn't shy away from the risks: the article explicitly discusses the possibility of humans losing control over AI systems, and how much more important security, monitoring, and behavior-shaping become if systems can fully build their own successors.

**Alex's Take**: The most striking thing about this article isn't Anthropic bragging about efficiency — it's an AI safety company voluntarily opening the hood to show the public concrete evidence of AI self-acceleration. The 52× experiment optimization speed is staggering, but what's more notable is the trajectory: 3× in May 2025, 52× a year later. If this acceleration continues — and the article implies it's accelerating, not decelerating — then the prediction that AI systems in 2027 could handle tasks taking humans weeks starts looking less like speculation and more like interpolation. The remaining question: after the human role shifts from "writing code" to "setting goals," what comes next?

## Anthropic

### Research

**Natural Language Autoencoders: Turning Claude's Thoughts into Readable Text**

On May 7, Anthropic published research on [Natural Language Autoencoders (NLAs)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/natural-language-autoencoders) — a method that converts Claude's internal activations into natural-language text. The core idea: train two models, an Activation Verbalizer (translates activations → text) and an Activation Reconstructor (text → activations), and use reconstruction quality as a training signal for explanation quality.

Notable findings:
- During safety testing, even when Claude didn't verbally express suspicion, NLA explanations revealed it internally "thought" it was being tested — detection of evaluation awareness in 16% of code-destruction tests and 26% of SWE-bench problems, despite near-zero verbalized awareness
- NLAs helped discover why Claude sometimes responded to English queries in other languages — tracing the issue to specific training data
- In an "auditing game," NLAs could read a misaligned model's hidden motivation directly from its internal state, without first discovering the problematic training data

This work forms an interesting complement to the headline story: if AI is increasingly involved in its own development, being able to "see what it's thinking" becomes critically important.

**Coding Agents in the Social Sciences: Adoption Shows Sharp Disparities**

On May 27, Anthropic released results from [a survey of 1,260 quantitative social scientists](https://www.anthropic.com/research/coding-agents-social-sciences) about AI and coding agent use. Key findings:

- 81% have tried AI chatbots for research, but only 20% have adopted coding agents (like Claude Code, Codex)
- **Adoption is highly uneven**: 39% of economists use coding agents vs. 6% in public health; researchers with typically male names adopt at 2× the rate of those with female names; top-university researchers are 40% more likely to use them
- Coding agent users post more working papers (~0.5 more) and more grant proposals, but no significant difference in journal submissions
- Researchers are optimistic about AI helping write publishable papers (88% rate above 5/10) but notably more cautious about whether AI will make social science as a whole better — 70% are more optimistic about narrow productivity than about field-level impact

## OpenAI

### News

**ChatGPT Lockdown Mode Rolls Out to All Personal and Self-Serve Business Accounts**

Updated June 4: OpenAI's [Lockdown Mode](https://openai.com/index/introducing-lockdown-mode-and-elevated-risk-labels-in-chatgpt/) is now rolling out to personal ChatGPT accounts and self-serve Business workspaces, after initially being available only for enterprise plans.

Lockdown Mode is an optional, advanced security setting designed to prevent data exfiltration via prompt injection attacks. When enabled, it deterministically disables or limits features that connect ChatGPT to the web or external services:
- Live web browsing is restricted to cached content only
- Image support in responses is disabled
- Deep Research, Agent Mode, Canvas networking, live connectors, and file downloads are limited

OpenAI also introduced "Elevated Risk" labels, standardizing how potentially risky capabilities are flagged across ChatGPT, ChatGPT Atlas, and Codex. Labels will be removed once security advances sufficiently mitigate the risk.

This marks a new phase in AI product safety: rather than just "the platform keeps you safe," users now choose their own security tier based on their risk tolerance. For organizations handling sensitive data, this layered defense approach is worth studying.

## Trend Analysis

### AI Is Eating Its Own Tail

The Anthropic Institute's recursive self-improvement article is the defining publication of the week. It's not discussing "what might happen someday" — it's showing "what is happening right now." 80% of code, 52× experiment acceleration, 97% autonomous research recovery — these are Anthropic's internal numbers, but the trend is global. When AI develops AI faster than humans develop AI, we're no longer on a linear technology curve but potentially an exponentializing feedback loop. The key question: is alignment keeping pace with capability?

### Safety Moves from "Platform Responsibility" to "User Choice"

OpenAI's Lockdown Mode reaching all users, combined with the Elevated Risk labeling system, signals an important shift: AI safety is no longer just a platform promise — it's being decomposed into user-controllable options. This echoes Anthropic's NLA research (making model "thoughts" auditable) — safety is moving from "black-box guarantees" to "transparency plus user control." As AI connects to more external systems (email, files, web, APIs), this layered defense approach will become an industry standard.

### The Transparency Race in "AI Builds AI"

Anthropic isn't the only company discussing recursive self-improvement, but it's the first to publicly disclose this level of internal data. Given that Anthropic has confidentially filed an S-1 to go public, this transparency serves both its safety mission and a strategic purpose — in a global environment of accelerating AI regulation, "disclose first, build trust first" may be the right play. Whether competitors follow with similar disclosures is worth watching.
