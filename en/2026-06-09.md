## Headline

### Anthropic: "What's Missing Before AI Agents Can Do Biology?"

**What happened**: On June 8, Anthropic published [Paving the way for agents in biology](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-in-biology), by Laura Luebbert and team. The authors open with an evocative metaphor: asking AI agents to navigate biological data infrastructure is like driving through an old Italian hill town — the streets may be beautiful, but they're narrow, winding, and designed for a completely different mode of transport.

**Key findings**:
- **VirBench**: The team built a benchmark of 120 real-world viral sequence queries across 40 pathogens, testing Claude Sonnet 4, Claude Opus 4.7, Biomni, Edison Analysis, and GPT-5.2-pro
- **Accuracy was alarmingly low**: The strongest models achieved mean accuracies ranging from 16.9% to 91.3% — for these tasks, the effective bar is 100%
- **Reproducibility was the killer problem**: The same model given the same query three times produced wildly different results. Sonnet 4 returned 106, 15, and 5 sequences for one Ebolavirus query (correct answer: 266)
- **gget virus: A deterministic retrieval layer**: The team developed a deterministic tool called gget virus. Once integrated, all agents exceeded 90% accuracy, with GPT-5.5 reaching 99.7%. The performance gap between models narrowed dramatically

**Why it matters**: This research reveals a critical, overlooked bottleneck — the constraint on scientific AI agents isn't just reasoning capability, but the absence of widespread deterministic data access layers. Using the ongoing Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in the DRC as a case study, researchers need to retrieve historical sequences from NCBI Virus to answer three urgent questions: How different is the outbreak virus? Can existing diagnostics still detect it? Will existing therapeutics still work? But the very first data retrieval step can go wrong, and wrong results can look "plausible" — the most dangerous failure mode in science.

**Alex's analysis**: This paper reminded me of Karpathy's complaint that "the code was the easiest part — most of the work was clicking around in browser dashboards." Biologists have been living in that click-hell for decades. Anthropic's finding is pragmatic: rather than waiting for models to get smart enough to navigate messy infrastructure, build them a deterministic highway first. The key insight from gget virus — once you add a deterministic layer, which model you use matters much less. That means democratizing scientific AI doesn't necessarily require the most expensive model — it requires better tools.

**Links**: [Original](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-in-biology) | [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06749)

## Anthropic

### Research

**Making Claude a Chemist: Claude Matches Specialized NMR Software**

On June 5, Anthropic published [Making Claude a chemist](https://www.anthropic.com/research/making-claude-a-chemist), the first systematic evaluation of Claude on chemistry tasks. Key findings:

- **Forward prediction (structure → spectrum)**: Opus 4.7 achieved ±0.079 ppm average error on hydrogen NMR — well under the ±0.20 ppm industry tolerance — surpassing ChemDraw and MestReNova. On carbon NMR, Opus 4.7 and MestReNova were effectively tied (±1.37 vs ±1.48 ppm)
- **Inverse prediction (spectrum → structure)**: Opus 4.7 correctly identified all 8 simpler target compounds on all 3 attempts; for 7 harder targets (fused rings, spirocycles), it succeeded on all 3 runs for 4 targets and 2 of 3 runs for the remaining 3
- **Significance**: A general-purpose model (no chemistry-specific fine-tuning) now rivals dedicated NMR software and can even do inverse structure elucidation — a task existing software leaves to the chemist

**Annual AI Cyber Threat Report: Attackers Going Deeper with AI**

On June 3, Anthropic released [What we learned mapping a year's worth of AI-enabled cyber threats](https://www.anthropic.com/news/AI-enabled-cyber-threats-mitre-attack), analyzing 832 accounts banned for malicious cyber activity between March 2025 and March 2026.

**Three key conclusions**:
1. **Attackers are using AI in more dangerous ways**: 67.3% used AI for writing malware; 6.5% for lateral movement. The share of medium-to-high risk actors jumped from 33% to 56% — a 1.7x increase
2. **Cyberattacks are becoming more autonomous**: AI can chain together attack stages, and traditional methods of distinguishing high- from low-risk actors are no longer effective
3. **MITRE ATT&CK needs updating**: Agentic orchestration of attacks (like the state-sponsored espionage operation disrupted in November 2025) has no corresponding classification in the existing framework

**Links**: [Original](https://www.anthropic.com/news/AI-enabled-cyber-threats-mitre-attack) | [Frontier Red Team Blog](https://red.anthropic.com/2026/attack-navigator/)

Jensen Huang's framing: "For forty years, you launched apps. Click. Type. With RTX Spark and Microsoft Windows, you ask — and the PC does the work."

**Alex's analysis**: RTX Spark isn't another GPU — it's NVIDIA's definition of the "personal AI computer" category. 1 Petaflop + 128GB unified memory means you can run 120B-parameter models locally with 1M-token context on a laptop. NVIDIA and Microsoft building OpenShell together is the key infrastructure to make personal agents go from concept to product. But the real signal is the ecosystem reaction — Adobe rewriting Photoshop, llama.cpp's founder endorsing it, XBOX expanding to RTX Spark devices. This isn't just NVIDIA's bet — it's a directional wager from the entire PC industry.

## Trend Watch

### 🔬 Scientific AI: From "Demo-able" to "Reliable"

Anthropic's two science-focused papers (biology agents + chemistry analysis) and OpenAI's GPT-Rosalind update point to a shared trend: AI is evolving from "can do demos in science" to "can do real work in science." The core bottleneck isn't model intelligence — it's data infrastructure and deterministic tools. The gget virus story shows that scientific AI breakthroughs may come more from "building roads" than "building faster cars."

### 💻 Year One of the Personal AI Computer

The NVIDIA RTX Spark + Microsoft Windows Open Shell combination, Adobe rewriting applications, and llama.cpp native support mark the transition of "personal AI computer" from concept to product. When laptops can run 120B models locally with 1M-token context, the "privacy" and "latency" barriers to AI agents are removed simultaneously.

### 🛡️ The Agentic Cybersecurity Era

Anthropic's analysis of 832 malicious accounts reveals a disturbing trend: AI isn't just making existing attacks easier — it's spawning entirely new attack patterns through autonomous chain orchestration. The MITRE ATT&CK framework hasn't caught up, and the security community needs new taxonomies for AI-era threats.

### 📊 Knowledge Workers: AI's New Growth Engine

OpenAI Codex reports 20% of users are non-developers, growing 3x faster. Meta launches Business Agent globally for all business sizes. This isn't coincidence — AI products are evolving from "tools for developers" to "coworkers for everyone."
