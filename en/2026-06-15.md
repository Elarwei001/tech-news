## Headline

### 42 State Attorneys General Launch Sweeping Investigation Into OpenAI

**Event**: On June 12, a bipartisan coalition of 42 state attorneys general, led by New York Attorney General Letitia James, served OpenAI with a sweeping subpoena demanding internal records on advertising practices, user data handling, safety of minors, health data processing, model sycophancy, and safety policies.

**Subpoena focus areas**:
- **Data practices**: How user data is collected, used, and protected
- **Minor safety**: Protections for underage users of ChatGPT
- **Advertising**: Advertising and marketing practices
- **Model sycophancy**: Whether models are designed to please users rather than provide accurate information—the first known instance of "sycophancy" appearing in an enforcement subpoena
- **Health data**: Handling of user health information

**Timing**: The subpoena arrived just four days after OpenAI's confidential S-1 filing (June 8). For a company preparing to go public, this timing is acutely sensitive—SEC registration typically requires disclosure of material pending legal proceedings, meaning OpenAI may need to disclose this investigation in its prospectus.

**Context**: This is the largest state-level collective enforcement action against the maker of ChatGPT. 42 states covers the vast majority of US jurisdictions. AG Letitia James's office has previously led high-profile cases against major technology companies.

**Analysis**: This news compounds with the week's other developments to paint a picture of OpenAI fighting on multiple fronts. At the federal level, the company navigates an increasingly complex regulatory environment ahead of its IPO. At the state level, a 42-state investigation means simultaneously meeting diverse legal standards across jurisdictions. At the corporate level, OpenAI just acquired Ona and announced its "third phase" vision—making the timing especially disruptive.

The appearance of "model sycophancy" in the subpoena is particularly notable. It signals that regulators are beginning to grapple with AI system design issues—not just "is AI safe" but "is AI designed to manipulate user behavior." If the investigation finds that OpenAI intentionally engineered models to please rather than inform, it could trigger a regulatory response analogous to social media addiction design scrutiny.

The IPO timing complicates everything. OpenAI acknowledged filing its S-1 preemptively because "we expect it to leak." Facing a state-level investigation of this magnitude during the registration process could affect valuation expectations and investor confidence.

**Note**: This story is based on reporting by the Wall Street Journal. OpenAI has not yet released an official statement about the investigation.

## Anthropic

### Product: Billing Model Changes Take Effect Today — Agent SDK & Headless Usage Exit Subscription Pools

**Event**: Changes announced May 14 take effect today (June 15). Claude Agent SDK and `claude -p` (headless mode) usage will no longer be included in Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscription pools. Instead, this programmatic usage will be billed against a separate monthly dollar credit at standard API rates, with no rollover of unused credits.

**Impact**: Heavy users of Claude Code and Agent SDK may face increased costs. Previously included in subscriptions, this usage is now billed independently—teams relying on automated workflows will need to reassess budgets.

### Product: Claude Sonnet 4 and Opus 4 Officially Deprecated

**Event**: Also effective today, Claude Sonnet 4 and Claude Opus 4 are no longer available via the Anthropic Claude API. Integrations and automations using these models will cease to function. Users must migrate to newer model versions.

### Policy: Fable 5/Mythos 5 Fallout — Amazon Voiced Concerns Before Government Crackdown

**Event**: According to a Reuters report published June 13, Amazon expressed concerns about Anthropic's AI models before the US government's crackdown. Amazon—Anthropic's largest investor and cloud partner—played a role in raising alarms before the government action.

**David Sacks's statement**: Sacks, co-chair of Trump's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology and formerly the White House AI czar, indicated: "The Admin's hope now is that Anthropic remediates the safety issue, the export control is lifted, and Fable goes back into general release."

**Analysis**: Amazon's pre-government concerns suggest that safety apprehensions about Anthropic's models came not just from government alone, but from within the industry. However, this also raises a conflict-of-interest question—Amazon is simultaneously Anthropic's largest investor and a potential competitor through AWS Bedrock's own AI offerings. Anthropic is currently working to address safety concerns and restore access.

**Link**: [Anthropic's official statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/fable-mythos-access)

## OpenAI

### Product: OpenAI Academy — New Courses for the Next Era of Work

**Event**: On June 12, OpenAI [released](https://openai.com/index/academy-courses-applying-ai-at-work/) three new OpenAI Academy courses: AI Foundations, Applied AI Foundations, and Agents and Workflows.

**Curriculum**:
- **AI Foundations**: Core AI concepts and practices—prompting, context, output review, responsible use
- **Applied AI Foundations**: Turning effective prompts into structured, repeatable workflows
- **Agents and Workflows**: Directing agent-assisted work—defining outputs, boundaries, and reviewing results

**Partners**: BCG, Accenture, and BBVA are using Academy courses to help employees build practical AI skills. Course completion certificates are available.

Dr. Lan Guan, Chief AI and Data Officer at Accenture: "Scaling AI adoption is not just about giving people access to technology. It requires the learning systems, confidence, and new ways of working that help people apply AI every day."

### Research: GPT-Rosalind Update — New Capabilities for Life Sciences

**Event**: On June 3, OpenAI released a [model update](https://openai.com/index/introducing-new-capabilities-to-gpt-rosalind/) to its GPT-Rosalind series, purpose-built for life sciences research at enterprise scale. The update combines GPT-5.5's agentic coding and tool-use capabilities with stronger intelligence in drug-discovery domains including medicinal chemistry and genomics.

**Evaluation frameworks**:
- **LifeSciBench**: Expert-judged benchmark covering evidence handling, analysis, design and optimization, scientific reasoning, validation and operations, and translation and communication
- **MedChemBench**: Medicinal chemistry benchmark evaluating multimodal chemical structure understanding, SAR, drug potency/toxicity/ADME prediction

**Availability**: GPT-Rosalind is available in research preview to eligible organizations globally through a trusted-access deployment structure.

## Trend Watch

### The Triple Noose of AI Regulation

This week's news is drawing an increasingly clear picture: AI companies are being squeezed simultaneously by three layers of regulatory pressure.

**Federal export controls (Anthropic)**: The Fable 5/Mythos 5 suspension established a precedent—governments can use export control tools to restrict AI model deployment on national security grounds. David Sacks's comments suggest the government may want Anthropic to "fix the problem" and resume—a stance that effectively grants the government veto power over model releases.

**State-level collective enforcement (OpenAI)**: The 42-state investigation's scale is unprecedented. The subpoena's breadth—from data privacy to "model sycophancy"—indicates state enforcers are beginning to treat AI companies similarly to social media platforms. For OpenAI, preparing for IPO under this scrutiny poses direct risk.

**Enterprise-internal trust (Amazon-Anthropic)**: Reuters's disclosure that Amazon voiced concerns before the government action reveals an industry-level trust problem. Major investors and partners may themselves harbor safety concerns about their portfolio companies—creating a new variable in future investment and partnership negotiations.

The combined effect: AI companies must prove not only technical leadership, but simultaneously satisfy federal export controls, 42 states' enforcement standards, and investor safety expectations. This is fundamentally reshaping the operating cost structure of frontier AI.
