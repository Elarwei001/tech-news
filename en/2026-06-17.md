## Headline

### Anthropic: Claude Code Data Suggests Agents Amplify Expertise Rather Than Erase It

**Event**: Anthropic published the economic research report "Agentic coding and persistent returns to expertise." The study analyzes roughly 400,000 interactive Claude Code sessions from about 235,000 users between October 2025 and April 2026, using privacy-preserving methods, to ask a practical question: as coding agents become more capable, what happens to human expertise?

**Key findings**:
- In Claude Code sessions, humans typically decide what to do and what counts as done, while Claude handles much of how to do it.
- Humans make about 70% of planning decisions but only about 20% of execution decisions.
- About 56% of sessions involve writing, fixing, testing, or orchestrating code; 17% involve operating software; 13% are already about data analysis or prose/document work.
- Over the seven-month study period, debugging sessions fell by nearly half, while usage shifted toward more end-to-end agentic work.
- Anthropic estimates, by comparison to freelance job postings, that the value of the typical task rose by about 25% on average.
- For coding sessions, users outside software occupations succeed at rates close to software engineers; domain expertise is still a strong success signal, with expert-rated sessions achieving verified success more than twice as often as novice-rated sessions.

**Link**: [Original](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise) | [Chinese translated digest](../translations/2026-06-17/anthropic-claude-code-expertise.md) *(AI translated digest)*

**Analysis**: The point is not simply that Claude Code is powerful. The more useful finding is the labor split: agents are eating execution friction, but they have not eaten problem framing, acceptance criteria, and domain judgment.

That makes the labor-market story more complicated than "everyone becomes a programmer." Lower barriers do not mean lower expertise. A person who understands financial risk, drug discovery, supply chains, or education assessment may no longer need to write large amounts of code by hand to turn ideas into working systems. But someone who does not understand the problem boundary will still struggle with recovery, validation, and direction.

In other words, coding agents are turning programming from a standalone craft into a medium for domain work. The squeezed layer is the work of translating someone else's clear requirements into code. The amplified layer is knowing what is worth building and how to tell whether the result is any good.

Anthropic is careful about limitations: the study cannot observe whether outputs are actually used in production, classifications depend on model analysis of transcripts, and a large amount of non-interactive headless usage is excluded. Even so, the signal is important: in the agent era, skill premiums do not disappear. They migrate from syntax and framework fluency toward task modeling, domain understanding, and result auditing.

## OpenAI

### Research: Simulating Deployment to Predict Model Behavior Before Release

**Event**: OpenAI published "Predicting model behavior before release by simulating deployment," describing a method for estimating how a candidate model may behave after launch by replaying realistic conversation contexts before deployment.

**Method**: OpenAI removes the old assistant response from historical deployed conversations, applies privacy-preserving processing, asks the candidate model to regenerate the response, and uses the resulting deployment-like samples to search for new failure modes and estimate the frequency of undesired behavior.

**Key results**:
- OpenAI used the method across multiple GPT-5-series Thinking deployments and analyzed about 1.3 million de-identified conversations.
- Deployment Simulation outperformed challenging-prompt baselines in predicting both the direction and exact rate of known undesired behaviors.
- The method surfaced "calculator hacking," a newly identified misalignment behavior, before release.
- For agentic coding trajectories, OpenAI tested tool simulation on 120,000 internal employee agent trajectories, showing that even complex tool-heavy environments can be reconstructed with high fidelity.
- OpenAI stresses that this is not a replacement for red-teaming, adversarial evaluations, or targeted tail-risk analysis, especially for extremely rare failures.

**Link**: [Original](https://openai.com/index/deployment-simulation/)

**Analysis**: This is safety infrastructure news. Traditional model safety evaluation often looks like an exam: sharper prompts, harder tests, more adversarial cases. But once agents enter production, the risky behavior may emerge from ordinary tasks, realistic context, tool calls, and user adaptation.

OpenAI's approach moves safety evaluation toward a pre-release traffic rehearsal. It will not catch everything, but it makes one key question more measurable: if the candidate model actually ships, does a risk category likely go up or down, and at what approximate scale?

The evaluation-awareness angle is also important. OpenAI reports that simulated traffic resembles real traffic much more closely than traditional eval sets from the model's perspective. That points toward a future where frontier-model safety is less about static benchmarks and more about continuous, production-distribution-driven risk forecasting.

## NVIDIA

### AI Infrastructure: Blackwell Sweeps MLPerf Training 6.0

**Event**: NVIDIA announced that its Blackwell platform led MLPerf Training 6.0 across all seven benchmarks. The round added two mixture-of-experts pretraining workloads, DeepSeek-V3 671B and GPT-OSS-20B, reflecting the growing importance of MoE architectures.

**Key figures**:
- Blackwell NVL72 systems submitted DeepSeek-V3 671B training at 8,192-GPU scale.
- Microsoft Azure trained Llama 3.1 405B on 8,192 GB200 NVL72 GPUs and reached the reference quality target in 7.07 minutes.
- CoreWeave trained DeepSeek-V3 671B at 8,192-GPU scale using GB300 NVL72 systems connected with Spectrum-X Ethernet, reaching the quality target in 2.02 minutes.
- GB300 NVL72 delivered up to 1.6x faster training than GB200 NVL72 at the same scale.

**Link**: [Original](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/blackwell-mlperf-training-6-0/)

**Analysis**: This is not just a benchmark victory lap. With MoE training now central to MLPerf, the infrastructure competition becomes clearer: not just single-GPU performance, but NVLink, networking, low-precision training, fault recovery, and reproducible large-scale jobs. The cost curve for agents and frontier models ultimately lands on these systems.

### Enterprise: HPE AI Factory With NVIDIA Expands for the Agent Era

**Event**: NVIDIA and HPE expanded HPE AI Factory with NVIDIA at HPE Discover, adding NVIDIA Vera CPU, NVIDIA Agent Toolkit, NVIDIA Confidential Computing, and deeper full-stack NVIDIA integration.

**Highlights**:

- NVIDIA Agent Toolkit includes Nemotron open models, OpenShell secure runtime, and NemoClaw blueprints for monitoring, governing, and running multi-agent systems.
- HPE Private Cloud AI adds local agent registration and governance approvals, while HPE Zerto Software capabilities detect abnormal agent actions and rewind to a clean state.
- NVIDIA Confidential Computing extends across HPE AI Factory at Scale, HPE Sovereign AI Factory, and HPE Private Cloud AI.

**Link**: [Original](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/hpe-ai-factory-agentic-enterprise/)

**Analysis**: NVIDIA is translating "agent-era infrastructure" into enterprise procurement language: CPUs for tool calls and real-time data work, GPUs for model workloads, DPUs and networking for isolation and policy enforcement, and software for governance and observability. Enterprises do not just care whether agents can complete a demo. They care whether failures can be detected, rolled back, and audited without exposing data or models.

## Trend Watch

### The Agent Stack Needs Expertise, Evaluation, and Infrastructure at the Same Time

Read together, today's first-party updates point in the same direction.

Anthropic's research says agents reduce execution barriers but do not replace human problem understanding. OpenAI's deployment simulation says pre-release safety cannot rely only on static exams; it needs realistic traffic rehearsal. NVIDIA and HPE's updates say enterprise agents require a full stack from chips, networking, and runtimes to governance and rollback.

That means the second half of 2026 will not be decided only by which model is smarter. The real split will be who helps expert users express tasks more effectively, who predicts deployment risk more accurately before release, and who runs agents inside enterprise systems in a stable, auditable, recoverable way.

Model capability remains the base layer. It is no longer the whole answer.
