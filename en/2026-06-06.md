## Headline

### Claude Becomes a Chemist: General-Purpose AI Matches Specialized NMR Software for the First Time

**Event**: On June 5, Anthropic published "[Making Claude a Chemist](https://www.anthropic.com/research/making-claude-a-chemist)," showcasing Claude's breakthrough performance in nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy — one of the most common and time-consuming analytical tasks in synthetic chemistry. Anthropic chemist David Kamber systematically evaluated three Claude models against dedicated NMR software on 20 novel compounds drawn from ChemRxiv preprints published after the models' training cutoff.

**Key Findings**:
- **Forward prediction**: Opus 4.7 achieved an average hydrogen (¹H) shift error of ±0.079 ppm — well under half the ±0.20 ppm tolerance window chemists consider correct. On carbon (¹³C), Opus 4.7 was effectively tied with MestReNova at ±1.37 vs. ±1.48 ppm
- **Inverse prediction (structure elucidation)**: Given only a molecular formula and 1D NMR spectra, Opus 4.7 recovered all 8 simpler structures on every attempt. On the 7 harder targets (fused rings, spirocycles), it got the correct structure on all 3 runs for 4 of them
- **No chemistry-specific fine-tuning**: Claude is a general-purpose model that matched or beat ChemDraw and MestReNova without any domain-specific training
- **Splitting pattern accuracy**: Opus 4.7 matched experimental splitting patterns more often than any other tool. All Claude models predicted sub-peak spacing to within half a hertz ~80% of the time, vs. 26–35% for ChemDraw and MestReNova

**Why It Matters**: NMR spectral interpretation is one of the most time-consuming steps in synthetic chemistry — for every compound, a chemist must match each peak in the spectrum to an atom in the proposed structure by hand. This research shows that a general-purpose frontier model can now handle not just forward prediction (structure → spectrum), but the harder reverse task (spectrum → structure) that existing dedicated software currently leaves to the chemist.

**Alex's Take**: This is a milestone that's easy to overlook. AI in chemistry has been hamstrung for years by data scarcity — sparse null results, inconsistent formats, paywalled journals — but multimodal frontier models are bypassing these limitations entirely. They can read chemical structures directly from journal figures and experimental spectra, with no pre-curated database required. The fact that Opus 4.7 outperforms all dedicated tools on splitting pattern prediction suggests the model may be building some kind of implicit representation of intermolecular interactions. If these results hold up across hundreds of compounds spanning 20–30 scaffold classes, this could be the inflection point for AI-assisted chemistry.

## Anthropic

### News

**Anthropic Publishes Year-Long Study of AI-Enabled Cyber Threats**

On June 3, Anthropic released a report analyzing 832 accounts banned for malicious cyber activity between March 2025 and March 2026, mapped against the MITRE ATT&CK framework. Three key findings: (1) threat actors are using AI in later, more complex stages of cyber operations; (2) attacks are becoming more autonomous, and old methods of assessing risk levels no longer work; (3) the MITRE ATT&CK framework doesn't fully capture AI-enabled behaviors.

The data is sobering: in the first six-month period, 33% of actors were classified as medium-risk or higher. By the second period, that share jumped to 56% — a 1.7-fold increase. 67.3% used AI for malware writing, but more concerning is the 6.5% who used AI for lateral movement — navigating deep inside compromised networks. AI-assisted phishing fell 8.6% while account discovery rose 8.9%, suggesting attackers are moving deeper into the attack lifecycle.

**Link**: [What we learned mapping a year's worth of AI-enabled cyber threats](https://www.anthropic.com/news/AI-enabled-cyber-threats-mitre-attack) | [Frontier Red Team analysis](https://red.anthropic.com/2026/attack-navigator/)

## Google DeepMind

### Research

**Gemma 4 12B: An Encoder-Free Multimodal Model for Laptops**

On June 3, Google DeepMind released Gemma 4 12B, a 12-billion-parameter open-weight multimodal model with a radical innovation: **no multimodal encoders**. Visual and audio inputs flow directly into the LLM backbone.

**Key Highlights**:
- Vision: replaced the vision encoder with a lightweight embedding module (single matrix multiplication + positional embeddings)
- Audio: removed the audio encoder entirely — raw audio signals are projected into the same dimensional space as text tokens
- **Runs locally on laptops with just 16GB RAM/VRAM**, with performance approaching the 26B MoE model
- Apache 2.0 license; supported by Hugging Face, Ollama, llama.cpp, MLX, vLLM, SGLang
- **First mid-sized Gemma model with native audio input**

The Gemma 4 family has surpassed 150 million downloads. This release further lowers the barrier for running high-quality multimodal agents on consumer hardware.

**Link**: [Introducing Gemma 4 12B](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/introducing-gemma-4-12b/) | [Developer Guide](https://developers.googleblog.com/gemma-4-12b-the-developer-guide/)

## Trend Analysis

### General-Purpose Models Are Eating Specialized Software

Anthropic's NMR research is a landmark event: a general-purpose model without chemistry-specific training matching commercial software in a highly specialized analytical domain. Google's Gemma 4 12B applies the same logic — strip out encoders, let the general architecture handle multimodal data natively. The trend is unmistakable: general foundation models are penetrating increasingly vertical professional domains, redefining workflows that traditionally depended on specialized software and domain expertise.

### AI Is Going from Tool to Teammate

OpenAI's Codex knowledge work report, Meta's Business Agent, and NVIDIA's RTX Spark all point in the same direction: AI is transitioning from a query-response tool to an autonomous teammate capable of executing multi-step workflows. 20% of Codex's 5M weekly active users are non-developer knowledge workers. Meta is positioning Business Agent as an "infinite team." And RTX Spark's core proposition is "you ask, and the PC does the work."

### The Cybersecurity Balance Is Tilting

Anthropic's 832-account analysis makes it clear: attackers are using AI in increasingly sophisticated ways, shifting from initial access to post-compromise operations. The medium-to-high-risk actor share grew 1.7x in six months. Meanwhile, MITRE ATT&CK hasn't caught up with agentic attack behaviors. On the defense side, Glasswing has uncovered 10,000+ critical vulnerabilities and OpenAI previewed its Daybreak cyber defense platform. But as Anthropic notes, the bottleneck has shifted from finding vulnerabilities to patching them — a fundamentally human-speed-limited process.
