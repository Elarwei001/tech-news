## Headline Deep Dive

### Anthropic RSP 3.3: stronger models require more institutionalized release constraints

**What happened**: Anthropic updated its [Responsible Scaling Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy) page on May 26 and published RSP 3.3. The RSP is Anthropic’s core governance framework for managing frontier-model risk, linking capability evaluations, AI Safety Levels, and organizational approval processes to training and deployment decisions.

**Why this leads today**: There was no single model launch that dominated the morning, but Anthropic’s RSP 3.3 is a more foundational signal. Over the past few days, Anthropic published the initial Project Glasswing update, showing Claude finding high-severity vulnerabilities in important software, and Chris Olah’s Vatican remarks, which argued that AI labs need external critics and broader social participation. RSP 3.3 brings that governance argument back inside the company: when models can be trained, deployed, or restricted should not be determined by product cadence alone.

**Key points**:

- Anthropic has updated its Responsible Scaling Policy to version 3.3 and provides the policy document through its official site.
- The RSP’s core idea is to raise safety, security, and deployment requirements as model capabilities increase.
- The framework focuses on high-risk capabilities, not only general benchmarks, including cyber, bio, autonomous behavior, and other capabilities that could amplify real-world risk.
- Anthropic uses AI Safety Levels to map model capabilities to required safeguards before training and deployment.
- The update connects with Anthropic’s recent Glasswing, frontier AI public-dialogue, and Vatican posts, showing safety moving from a research issue into organizational governance and public trust.

**Analysis**:

The importance of RSP 3.3 is not that it reads like a conventional news event. It matters because it answers a sharper question: as model capability keeps rising, who gets to let these systems enter the real world?

For the last two years, the AI race has often been framed as a capability race: better reasoning, longer context, stronger coding, lower latency, richer multimodality. But once systems become agentic, capability itself becomes a risk multiplier. A model that writes code is an assistant. A model that can call tools, read and write repositories, execute commands, plan attack paths, or automate experimental workflows becomes part of a real operating system.

That is why policies like the RSP matter. Model companies cannot simply say after launch that they care about safety. Real governance has to sit upstream in training, evaluation, red-teaming, deployment approval, permissioning, and high-risk-use restrictions. The RSP’s value depends on whether it becomes a hard constraint. If a model reaches a higher-risk capability threshold, will the release actually slow down? If mitigations are insufficient, will deployment be delayed? If outside society raises a credible concern, does the policy provide an auditable path for response?

Anthropic’s recent updates should be read together. Glasswing shows Claude can accelerate security discovery, but faster discovery makes verification, disclosure, and remediation the next bottlenecks. The Vatican remarks acknowledge that AI labs operate under commercial, research, geopolitical, and reputational incentives and therefore need critics outside the lab. RSP 3.3 is the internal-institutional answer: the company is writing the chain from capability to risk to constraint into policy.

Today’s read: Anthropic is turning safety from a brand claim into a competitive dimension. Frontier AI companies will no longer differ only on model leaderboards. They will differ on credible capability evaluation, clear deployment thresholds, audit mechanisms, and willingness to let external voices affect high-risk release decisions.

Today’s read: AI companies are moving from launching powerful models to operating powerful models.
